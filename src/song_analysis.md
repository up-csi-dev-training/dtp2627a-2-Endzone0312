## Song Analysis

“What’s Going On” (1971) is a protest song that argues through tenderness instead of anger. Marvin Gaye wrote it with Al Cleveland and Renaldo “Obie” Benson after Benson witnessed police violence against anti-war protesters, and Gaye’s own brother had returned from Vietnam, which made the subject personal. The lyrics address family figures (mother, father, brother) and call for love over war, for understanding across the generation gap, and for dialogue in place of brutality. The chorus pleads with listeners to talk and see what is really happening rather than meet protest with force. The song’s power comes from the contrast between its heavy subject matter and its smooth, gentle arrangement with layered vocals, which makes the message read as grief and concern rather than rage. Its central questions remain relevant, which helps explain why it is consistently ranked among the greatest songs ever recorded.
